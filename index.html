The science, briefly

What actually makes a V-taper

The V-taper is a ratio: how wide your shoulders and upper back are compared with how wide your waist is. You can push it two ways, by widening the top and by keeping the waist small.

MuscleEffect on the VPlan
Side delts (the outer part of the shoulder)The single biggest lever for shoulder width from the front and back. They sit on the outside edge of your frame.Top priority 3 days out of every 4 (Push, Legs + shoulders, Abs). Small muscle, recovers fast.
Lats (the big wing-shaped back muscles under your armpits)Make the upper back flare out and make the waist look smaller by contrast.Top priority Pull day: rows plus pullovers.
Rear delts (back of the shoulder)Round out the shoulder from the side and back, and help posture so your shoulders sit wide instead of rolled forward.Build Pull and Legs + shoulders days.
Upper chestFills out the top of the V from the front.Build Push day: 4 chest exercises, 2 on the incline.
Triceps and bicepsSmall role. Bigger arms widen the silhouette slightly.Build 3 exercises each, on Push and Pull days.
LegsNot part of the V, but a big upper body on thin legs looks off. They do not hurt the taper.Keep Half of the Legs + shoulders day, plus calves.
Upper traps (the muscle between your neck and shoulder)When they get big, the neck-to-shoulder line slopes up, which makes the shoulders look narrower.Don't target They still get enough from rows and Romanian deadlifts.
Obliques (the side abs)Heavily loaded oblique work can thicken the sides of the waist in some people.No heavy loading Bodyweight only, on the Abs day.

An honest note on the evidence: studies on attractiveness measure the whole shoulder-to-waist or chest-to-waist ratio. The muscle-by-muscle advice above is the consensus of coaches, not something tested in a lab. There are also no controlled studies on oblique work widening the waist. The cautious reading is that light or bodyweight core work is fine and heavy loaded side work is the risk. Your waist size mostly comes from body fat and your bone structure, so the fat part matters more than any ab exercise (see the last section).

Exercises that work against the V

The twisting move you were thinking of is almost certainly the Russian twist. Here is the full list to skip when it shows up in a video.

Form

The cues that matter most

Lateral raise

  • Go light. 10 to 20 lb is normal even for strong people. If you feel it in your neck, the weight is too heavy.
  • Lead with your elbows, not your hands. Keep a small bend in the elbow.
  • Stop at shoulder height. Going higher hands the work to your traps.
  • Keep your shoulders pressed down away from your ears the whole time.
  • Arms slightly in front of your body, not straight out to the side.
  • Lower over 2 to 3 seconds. No swinging or leaning back.

Single-arm row

  • Knee and hand on the bench, back flat and roughly parallel to the floor.
  • Pull your elbow toward your hip (your back pocket), not straight up to your chest. That is what makes it a lat exercise.
  • At the bottom, let the shoulder stretch down for a full lat stretch.
  • Don't twist your torso open or shrug at the top.

Pullover

  • Hold one dumbbell with both hands, elbows slightly bent and kept at that angle.
  • Lower behind your head only until you feel a stretch under your armpits.
  • Keep your ribs down; don't let your lower back arch up off the bench.
  • Start light. The bottom position puts the shoulder in a long stretch.
  • Never go to failure on this one. Stop 2 to 3 reps short.
  • Stuck rep: bend your elbows a lot so the dumbbell comes closer to your head, then pull it back over your chest. Never let go of a dumbbell above your face.
  • Adjustable dumbbells: hold the handle with both hands, not the end plates, and make sure the selector is fully locked before every set.
  • Safest version: lie on the floor. The floor catches the dumbbell at the bottom, so it can't drop past a safe stretch (floor pullover demo).

Rear delt fly

  • Chest down on an incline bench, or bent forward at about 90 degrees.
  • Move your arms out wide, not back toward your hips.
  • Do not squeeze your shoulder blades together hard; that shifts work to the mid-back and traps.
  • Light weight, slow lowering.

Dumbbell presses

  • Shoulder blades pulled back and down into the bench before you start.
  • Elbows at about 45 degrees from your body, not flared straight out.
  • Get heavy dumbbells into place by resting them on your knees and rolling back with them.
  • Feet flat on the floor, controlled lowering to chest level.

Romanian deadlift

  • Push your hips back; knees bend only slightly.
  • Dumbbells slide down the front of your thighs.
  • Back stays flat. Stop when your hamstrings are fully stretched or right before your back starts to round.
  • Stand tall at the top. Don't shrug.

Bulgarian split squat

  • Set your front foot by sitting on the bench and extending one leg; that is about where your heel goes. Then move it 2 to 3 inches out to the side.
  • Rest the top of your back foot (laces) on the bench, not just your toes.
  • Front knee follows the direction of your toes. Use a wall for balance at first.

Every session

  • Warm up: 5 minutes moving, then 1 to 2 light sets of the first exercise.
  • Two sets per exercise, taken to failure: the point where you can't do another clean rep. Presses, skull crushers, overhead extensions and goblet squats stop 1 rep short; pullovers stop 2 to 3 short. Adjustable dumbbells should not be dropped, so never fail a rep you can't set down safely. Form breaking down means the set is over.
  • On lateral raises and rear delt flys, failure means the first shrug. Shrugging hands the work to the upper traps, which make shoulders look narrower.
  • Sharp or joint pain: stop that exercise. Muscle burn is fine; joint pain is not.
  • Rest 2 to 3 minutes on rows, presses and leg work, 1 to 1.5 minutes on raises and arm work.
How to keep growing

Progress and the 50 lb ceiling

  • Rep ranges. Each exercise has a range like 8 to 12. Pick a weight you can do for the low end. When you hit the top of the range on every set, go up one dumbbell step (2.5 to 5 lb).
  • Write it down. A notes app is enough. Beat last time by one rep or one weight step.
  • When 50 lb gets easy (legs will hit this first): lower over 3 to 4 seconds, pause 2 seconds at the bottom, do "one and a half" reps (go down, half up, back down, all the way up), or push the rep range up to 20 to 30. All of these keep muscles growing with lighter weight.
  • Rest days. Each muscle group gets about 3 days off before its next turn. If a muscle is still sore enough to change how you move, or you feel run down, take a rest day (keep the steps) and do the next day in the cycle afterwards.
The other half of the ratio

Keeping the waist small

Most of your waist width is body fat and the width of your hip bones. You cannot spot-reduce fat with ab exercises, so the waist side of the V comes from the kitchen.

  • If you carry belly fat: eat a little under maintenance (roughly 300 to 500 calories below) while you train. You will lose fat from the waist and keep or build muscle up top.
  • Protein: about 0.7 to 1 gram per pound of bodyweight every day.
  • Check it weekly: once a week, same time of day (first thing in the morning is easiest), log your weight and your waist measured at the belly button. If neither has moved in 2 to 3 weeks and you still carry belly fat, eat a little less. The workout log has boxes for both.
  • Steps: 7,000 a day, rest days included. He found no visible difference going up to 10,000. Any cardio of the same length counts.
  • Sleep: 7 to 9 hours. This is when you recover from daily training.
